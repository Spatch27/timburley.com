Methodology & Decision Governance

A process built to give your team confidence in the choice.

Great names rarely fail because nobody had a good idea. They fail because the room didn’t know how to evaluate them, couldn’t agree on who decided, or left trademark reality until the end.

STAGE 01

Brief & Approval Pipeline

Before a single word is written, we lock down what the name must achieve, the competitive context, and—crucially—the decision pipeline: who contributes, who agrees, and who holds final sign-off.

We also agree clear evaluation criteria upfront so later discussions stay strategic rather than drifting into personal taste.

STAGE 02

Strategic Territories

Rather than jumping straight to random lists, we define distinct conceptual territories—exploring the range from descriptive clarity to evocative, coined or place-rooted naming.

This gives stakeholders a shared language for why a name works, not just whether they like the sound of it on first hearing.

STAGE 03

Name Creation & Early Screening

Human craft, deep exploration and ruthless editing. Every candidate is checked against real-world usage, linguistic fit, domain landscape and preliminary trademark conflicts before it ever reaches your table.

No AI buckets of five hundred generic words—only disciplined, curated options with real legs.

STAGE 04

Legal Partnership & Clearance

I work directly alongside your trademark attorney or IP firm, preparing clean shortlists and backup candidates in the right classes and territories.

That preparation avoids wasted legal searches and gives your lawyers what they need to get registration through effectively and cost-effectively.

STAGE 05

Selection & Supporting Narrative

Choosing the name is a moment of alignment, not exhaustion. I guide the final selection conversation and help articulate the launch narrative—so the name lands with conviction inside your business and out in the market.

Trademark Pragmatism

How naming and legal counsel work best together.

A great name that cannot be registered isn’t an asset—it’s a future legal bill.

I am not a trademark lawyer, and formal legal clearance always belongs with your IP firm. What separates this practice from general agencies is a deep, practical understanding of how trademark registration is actually achieved: distinctiveness thresholds, class overlaps, phonetic similarity and territorial risk.

By feeding your legal partner well-screened, strategically viable candidates—with strong backups already aligned internally—you spend far less on dead-end searches and reach a registered name far sooner.
