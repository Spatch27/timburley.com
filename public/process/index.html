Approach · Process · Pitfalls

How I think about names, and how we get one registered.

Approach and process are two different things. My approach sets the creative and commercial standard for what makes a name endure. My process is the disciplined route from strategy through deep exploration, small-room alignment and independent legal clearance.

01 · The Approach ↓ 02 · The Process ↓ 03 · Six Pitfalls ↓
01 · The Approach

Four types of name, explored with an open mind.

01 · Functional & Descriptive e.g. British Airways

Says plainly what it is.

The most common type of name, and usually the hardest to trademark because it occupies crowded, generic territory. Still, descriptive isn’t automatically wrong when the context is distinctive. Even Tim Burley is a functional name, stating plainly who does the work while standing apart.

02 · Experiential e.g. Monarch Airlines

Captures what it feels like to be a customer.

Reflects a defining quality of the service or customer experience, suggesting how the organisation treats people, like making every passenger feel like royalty.

03 · Evocative e.g. Virgin Atlantic

Connects through metaphor, attitude or idea.

Takes a lateral route into the brand rather than describing the category literally. Evocative names sit apart from sector clichés, creating something far more distinctive and far harder for competitors to copy.

04 · Invented e.g. Flybe

A blank slate that never existed before.

Coined words are usually the easiest to trademark. That blank canvas is both an advantage and a responsibility: you build all the meaning from scratch rather than leaning on existing associations.

The Four Watchwords

Simple, memorable, meaningful, and protectable.

01

Simple

Effortless on first hearing. Asking a single word to say everything almost always ends in something convoluted.

02

Memorable

Holds attention in the brief seconds you have to make a vocal first connection, and stays in the mind afterwards.

03

Meaningful

Anchored to strategy, while letting design, imagery and supporting language carry their share of the brand story.

04

Protectable

Registrable, or genuinely uncontested, turning the name into a business asset you can defend for its lifetime.

Creative leadership and process leadership matter equally. Trademark law is rarely black and white: plenty of names initially flagged as difficult have gone on to achieve registration through experienced legal counsel. Knowing how to navigate that path keeps the strongest options on the table.

02 · The Process

From strategy to registered name, in five disciplined stages.

01

Strategy & territories

The brief

We begin with the closing strategy brief, drawing on business strategy (including brand architecture and wider naming systems) and brand strategy (personality, character and emotional truth) to define distinct strategic territories to explore.

Not every theme belongs inside the name itself. Design, imagery and supporting language carry their share. However much you admire Starbucks’ symbol, it’s still Starbucks.

Risk coveredTrying to force one name to do everything, and losing people in the first two seconds.

02

Long-listing

~1,000 → ~100

From those territories I mind-map deeply, often up to 1,000 candidates. The best names almost always live in one of two fertile spaces: the compelling first expression right out of the brief, or two to three steps of separation once all the obvious ideas are exhausted.

Business owners tackling naming alone often get stuck swimming in the shallow end. Long-listing pushes into uncharted territory before I edit ruthlessly down to around 100 serious contenders, which you never have to wade through.

Risk coveredGoing round in circles in the shallow end and settling for tired sector clichés.

03

Shortlisting

12–15 names

Before anything reaches your table, I run practical knockout checks (Google and LLM searches, sector media, UK IPO database flags and domain availability) to rule out obvious dead ends.

What I present is a curated shortlist of 12 to 15 viable options, each with a concise rationale, domain availability view and any early trademark points worth noting.

Risk coveredFalling in love with unavailable names or wasting legal budget on obvious clashes.

04

Selection

3–4 favourites

We present and discuss the shortlist with the smallest possible deciding group the organisation will accept, avoiding committee-led compromises that quietly kill strong brand names.

Out of the 12 to 15 candidates, typically 3 or 4 options emerge that the room is genuinely excited to explore (and sometimes one clear favourite).

Risk coveredDecision-by-committee diluting the choice down to the safest, least-memorable name.

05

Clearance & filing

1 registered name

With favoured options agreed, we commission a formal pre-registration report from your trademark attorney, or an independent IP partner I introduce (paid directly by you and acting strictly for you). Depending on urgency, we check either the front-runner first or 3–4 candidates in parallel to avoid stop-start delays.

Once a strong path to registration is confirmed, I support your legal partner through filing, talk candidly about the commercial risks of starting design work before registration completes, and stay alongside you through to a registered name ready for market.

Risk coveredBlind design spend, stalled launches or expensive post-launch rebrands.
