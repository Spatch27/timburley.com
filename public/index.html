02 · Point of View

Coming up with a name is only half of the job.

HALF ONE · THE NAME

Judgement over volume.

Effective naming is not a word-generation game. It requires strategic discipline, linguistic sensitivity and an early understanding of how trademark registration actually works across classes and territories.

Whether the answer is an invented mark, a rooted place name, or an unmistakable plain-English phrase, the craft is knowing what will endure for the lifetime of the business.

HALF TWO · THE DECISION

Confidence without compromise.

Without a structured process, internal naming discussions quickly collapse into “who likes what best?” That usually ends in stalemate, or worse, the safe middle ground: the least-worst option everyone can grudgingly accept.

Twenty years of running naming projects, backed by a background leading agency teams towards positive outcomes, taught me that steering the room through evaluation, alignment and clearance is where a naming practice truly pays for itself.

06 · Clear Boundaries

Four things this practice doesn’t do.

  • No AI name-slop buckets I don’t feed prompts into an LLM and hand you 400 derivative words hoping one sticks. Naming takes human taste, commercial context and judgement.
  • No junior hand-offs There is no account-layer telephone game. You brief, challenge and decide directly with me from start to finish.
  • No amateur legal advice Formal trademark registration belongs with a qualified trademark attorney. My role is to understand the register deeply enough to give your IP partner clean, registrable candidates.
  • No bloated 90-slide theatre Just clear strategic criteria, disciplined territories, rigorously edited shortlists and straight-talking guidance through the decision.
